Enter the current logic area, active power, and converged frequency, then choose the source and target foundry/node. The tool estimates area, power, converged frequency, yield, and cost per good die for the target node and nearby nodes. Built-in defaults cover TSMC, Samsung, Intel, SMIC, GlobalFoundries/GF, and Huawei τ at available 28/22/16/14/12/10/7/6/5/4/3/2nm-class nodes. Low-confidence and mapped placeholder processes are removed from the selectable dataset.

Huawei LogicFolding Slide Validation

Calibration from the uploaded Huawei slides. These values are treated as roadmap / case-study parameters, not public PDK signoff.
Chip Density CalibrationThe LogicFolding density slide uses 2025 as 1×, then 2026 = 1.6×, 2027 = 1.7×, and 2029 = 1.8×. The Huawei τ entries now keep the raw 2T MTR, but area scaling uses the slide-calibrated standardized/effective MTR: 2025 103.3, 2026 165.3, 2027 175.7, 2029 186.0 MTr/mm².
DSP Case Cross-CheckThe DSP slide shows LogicFolding PPA gains of die area −40%, clock frequency +37%, buffer count −56%, wire length −25%, wire capacitance −34%, and total power −24%. The −40% area result corresponds to about 1.67× density, validating the 2026 1.6× chip-density anchor.
XPU PPA Cross-CheckThe performance/power slide reports 2026/2027 gains by workload: CPU single-core +15%/+44%, CPU multi-core +24%/+56%, GPU +38%/+87%, NPU +140%/+213%; power-efficiency gains are CPU +12%/+34%, GPU +40%/+78%, NPU +81%/+118%. These are stored in Huawei τ node notes; default frequency still follows the P-core roadmap.
W2W Yield FormulaThe cost/yield slide states YFolding = YTop × YBottom × YBonding. The tool now computes Huawei τ stack yield as layerYieldN × bondingYieldN−1, with a configurable 2-/3-layer W2W stack and bonding-yield input.

Model Sources & Limits

Numbers in this page are default estimation parameters, not foundry quotes or PDK signoff results.
Method Backbone: Scaling FactorsBased on Stillmaker & Baas 2017 CMOS scaling equations and the DeepScaleTool 2021 idea of fitting PPAC trends from public silicon data, then converting a design with scaling factors. This page implements three normalized factor groups: relative density, same-frequency energy, and converged frequency.
TSMCDefault N7/N6, N5/N4, N3, and N2 PPA anchors come from public TSMC pages/releases. N2 SRAM defaults use the roughly 38 Mb/mm² SRAM macro figure reported by TSMC Research. Less-public 16/14/12/10nm points are interpolated.
SamsungDefault SF7, SF5, SF4, and SF3 PPA anchors come from Samsung Foundry logic-node pages and releases; removed low-confidence SF2 placeholders from the selectable dataset.
IntelDefault Intel 4, Intel 3, and Intel 18A factors come from public Intel Foundry materials. Nominal 14/10/7/6/5/4/3/2nm labels map to Intel 14, 10SF/Intel 7, Intel 4, Intel 3, and 18A classes; they are not exact nm equivalents.
SMIC28nm, 14nm, and N+1/N+2-class points are anchored to public reporting and known service nodes. Unverified extrapolated SMIC placeholders have been removed from the selectable dataset.
GlobalFoundries / GFAdded only production-proven, non-placeholder nodes: 28SLP/SLPe, 22FDX FD-SOI, 14LPP FinFET, and 12LP/12LP+. GF 22FDX is kept as a true 22nm FD-SOI point and is not mapped to 16/14nm; GF 7nm and beyond remain excluded because the public 7nm program was put on hold.
Cost / YieldYield uses a negative-binomial model. Cost uses gross dies per wafer, die yield, line yield, package/test, and NRE amortization. Two IBS-style uploaded tables are supported. Manufacturing wafer cost is checked against the 50K-WPM tooling table: 28=$1,789, 16=$2,618, 7=$3,492, 5=$4,637, 4=$6,285, 3=$8,538, 2=$11,809. Intro wafer price is checked against the transistor-cost table: 28=$3,500, 16=$5,708, 10=$8,389, 7=$9,965, 5=$15,022, 4=$17,128, 3=$20,124.6, 2=$28,750. Choose the cost basis in Yield & Cost; missing 14/12/6 are interpolated.
Wafer Yield MapThe wafer map uses full rectangular dies inside the printable wafer radius, negative-binomial die yield, optional die-level PPM failures, edge defect boost, and deterministic pseudo-random pass/fail rendering. Huawei τ stack mode supports 2-/3-layer W2W: total cost scales with layer count and final stack pass follows the slide formula Yfolding = Ytop × Ybottom × Ybonding, generalized as layerYield^N × bondingYield^(N−1).
IBS Cost BaselinesManufacturing cost baseline: 28nm $1,789; 16nm $2,618; 7nm $3,492; 5nm $4,637; 4nm $6,285; 3nm $8,538; 2nm $11,809; interpolated 14nm $2,813, 12nm $3,024, 10nm $3,249, 6nm $4,024. Intro wafer price baseline from the transistor-cost table: 28nm $3,500; 16nm $5,708; 10nm $8,389; 7nm $9,965; 5nm $15,022; 4nm $17,128; 3nm $20,124.6; 2nm $28,750; interpolated 14nm $6,601.67, 12nm $7,495.33, 6nm $12,493.50. Huawei τ W2W multiplies the selected single-wafer baseline by the selected stack layer count.
IBS Die-Cost Cross-CheckThe intro-price table is internally consistent as wafer price / net dies per wafer: 28nm $3,500 / 465.08 ≈ $7.53, 16nm $5,708 / 386.85 ≈ $14.75, 10nm $8,389 / 564.22 ≈ $14.87, 7nm $9,965 / 388.88 ≈ $25.62, 5nm $15,022 / 466.80 ≈ $32.18, 4nm $17,128 / 443.04 ≈ $38.66, 3nm $20,124.6 / 444.58 ≈ $45.27, 2nm $28,750 / 448.06 ≈ $64.17.

Recommended use: use this tool for early architecture/technology trade-offs. During a real project, replace defaults with the target foundry PDK, standard-cell library, SRAM compiler, floorplan, STA, power, IR-drop, and thermal signoff results.
